# 5G Technology Overview

## Introduction to 5G Technology and Its Main Advantages

5G, or fifth-generation technology, is the newest standard for broadband cellular networks ( wireless networks that provide high-speed internet access ). Cellular phone companies started deploying 5G worldwide in 2019, planning for it to succeed the 4G networks that connect most current cell phones. Experts predict that by 2025, over 1.7 billion people globally will subscribe to 5G. Like previous generations, 5G uses a cellular network ( a radio network distributed over land areas called cells ), where each cell covers a small geographical area. ## 5G Network Pros: Small Cells, High Frequencies, Speed, and Latency

One major advantage of 5G networks is their use of small-cell technology ( smaller, low-power base stations that cover a shorter range ). Unlike 4G networks that rely on large towers, 5G uses small cells placed closer together. This ensures efficient communication and allows for denser data transmission within a smaller area. Another key benefit is that 5G uses high-frequency signals ( radio waves with a high oscillation rate ). These signals travel faster than the low-frequency signals used by older networks, contributing to overall speed. The 5G network also offers significantly faster connectivity. This will greatly improve mobile internet speeds, making downloads several times quicker. For example, a 10-minute download could take only about one minute with 5G. Furthermore, 5G wireless networks have minimal latency ( the delay before a data transfer begins ). This means there will be almost no delays when acquiring information, which is excellent for real-time applications like gaming, where buffering will be largely eliminated.

## Challenges with 5G Network Infrastructure and Cost

For a 5G network to work effectively, it needs to use different types of frequencies. As a result, these frequencies will require different carriers, which could lead to increased levels of radiation ( energy released in the form of electromagnetic waves or particles ). There is a need for better EMF protection ( protection against electromagnetic fields ). Currently, technology companies are still working on how to combine and synchronize these different frequencies. Another challenge is the short travel distance ( the limited range of high-frequency signals ). Although high-frequency signals travel faster, they can only go a short distance. Therefore, 5G networks will require many antennas, inputs, and outputs, all spread out over short distances, to ensure full coverage. To ensure a stable 5G connection, especially in buildings, many access points ( devices that allow wireless devices to connect to a network ) will be needed. The network's speed is the most important factor for connectivity, so having multiple access points helps achieve this. However, this also adds to the complexity. Another drawback is that 5G networks are currently more expensive. Most companies have not yet announced the exact cost of using 5G, but advanced technologies typically come with a higher price tag. It is expected that 5G will be more costly than 4G networks due to the advanced innovations involved.

## 5G Availability, Device Compatibility, and Health Concerns

Currently, 5G is not widely available around the world. Its presence is limited, especially in areas with low populations. The number of access points ( connection points for wireless devices ) will significantly affect availability. It is unlikely that carriers will quickly switch all their customer locations to 5G due to the major infrastructure changes required. Additionally, most existing devices are compatible with 4G and 3G networks, with some even still using 2G. Therefore, if 5G were fully available today, most devices would not be able to use it. Companies will need to manufacture new models that are compatible with 5G wireless networks and their frequencies. The power and speed of 5G networks may also drain device batteries faster, requiring manufacturers to upgrade battery technology. Lastly, several studies suggest that 5G could increase radiofrequency (RF) radiation ( energy in the electromagnetic spectrum, including radio waves ) levels near antennas. For example, research on electromagnetic fields in 2011 by the IARC reviewed the effects of RF radiation on humans and animals. This review confirmed that RF radiation can be carcinogenic ( causing cancer ) to humans. In essence, 5G, as a significant source of radiofrequency radiation, has the potential to cause harmful effects to people, animals, and the environment.
