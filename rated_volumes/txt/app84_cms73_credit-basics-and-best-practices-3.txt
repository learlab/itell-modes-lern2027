# Understanding Credit and Best Practices

## What is Credit and Its Importance?

Credit allows individuals to make significant purchases by obtaining loans, with the promise of future repayment. Lenders prefer to extend credit to those with a high likelihood of repayment. Building a positive credit history is crucial, as it aids in securing loans and impacts other services like insurance and utilities. Companies assess credit history to gauge risk and repayment ability. Healthy financial habits are essential for establishing and maintaining good credit.

## Understanding Your Credit Score

Your credit score is a three-digit number (e.g., FICO® 300-850) that predicts your credit behavior. A higher score indicates less risk, attracting lenders, while a lower score can lead to denials or higher interest rates. The FICO® score typically ranges from 300 to 850. Scores between 300 and 500 often indicate 'Room for Improvement,' 550 to 650 are considered 'Fair,' 700 to 800 are 'Good,' and scores reaching 850 are 'Excellent.' Key factors influencing your score include payment history , the amount of debt owed , the length of your credit history , new or recent credit , and the types of credit used . Your score is dynamic, changing with account activity and payments.

## The Importance of Your Credit Report

In addition to your score, your credit report details your credit activity and financial standing. It contains personal information, and comprehensive credit account details, including balances, limits, payment history, and creditor names. Public records, such as tax liens and bankruptcies, can also appear for 7-10 years. Independent credit bureaus like Experian, TransUnion, and Equifax compile and maintain these reports, making them vital for monitoring financial health.

## Checking and Correcting Your Credit Report

Regularly checking your credit report for errors is crucial. You are entitled to one free copy annually from each major credit bureau via annualcreditreport.com or by phone, requiring identity verification. If errors are found, first contact the reporting company/lender. If unresolved, formally dispute inaccurate information with the credit bureau, providing documentation. Be cautious of services promising to "fix" correctly reported negative items for a fee; legitimate disputes apply only to incorrect information, which by law, creditors and furnishers must correct or remove without third-party intervention.

## Best Practices for Establishing and Maintaining Good Credit

Freddie Mac recommends key behaviors for good credit. On-time payments are paramount. Keep your debt load manageable , using a small portion of your credit limit . Pay more than the minimum due, ideally the full amount. Annually review your credit reports for errors. Avoid "shopping for too much credit" (opening multiple new accounts quickly), which can negatively impact your score. These practices build a robust financial standing.

## Tips for Improving Your Credit Score

To further improve your score, Freddie Mac advises paying credit card bills in full and never charging more than affordable. Utilize only a small percentage of your credit limit . Focus on paying down existing debts . Strategically open and keep credit accounts in your name to build history, but avoid opening too many at once. Continue to dispute inaccuracies promptly. Improving credit demands patience and wise, consistent management.
