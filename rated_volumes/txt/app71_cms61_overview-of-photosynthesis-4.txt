Overview of Photosynthesis

This volume introduces photosynthesis, the vital process by which plants and other organisms convert light energy into chemical energy, creating sugars and oxygen. It details the stages of photosynthesis, the key cellular structures involved like chloroplasts, and the importance of this process for life on Earth.

# Overview of Photosynthesis

## Photosynthesis: How Plants Make Food

Solar-powered devices use energy from the sun. In a similar way, you also get your energy indirectly from the sun. The energy you need to play sports or read comes from ATP ( adenosine triphosphate ), which is an energy-carrying molecule. ATP is often made from the breakdown of sugars. But how are these sugars created? Plants capture some of the sun's energy and change it into chemical energy stored in sugars. Photosynthesis ( a process that captures light energy to make sugars ) is how plants make food. Organisms that perform photosynthesis are called producers because they create their own chemical energy. This energy then becomes the source for themselves and for most other organisms on Earth. For example, animals that eat plants get their energy directly from them. Animals that eat other animals get their energy indirectly from plants. The sugars and other carbon-based molecules in plants are made through photosynthesis. Almost all organisms rely on sunlight, directly or indirectly, for energy. Sunlight contains different types of radiant energy, including ultraviolet radiation, microwaves, and the visible light that we see. Plants absorb visible light for photosynthesis. Visible light appears white, but it is actually made up of several colors, or wavelengths. Inside plant cells, in structures called chloroplasts , there is a molecule called chlorophyll ( KLAWR-uh-fihl ). Chlorophyll absorbs some of the energy from visible light. There are two main types, chlorophyll a and chlorophyll b. Together, they mostly absorb red and blue light wavelengths. They don't absorb much green light. Plants also have other molecules that absorb green light, but in smaller amounts. Because chlorophyll reflects green light, plants appear green. ! ## Photosynthesis in Plant Cells: The Chloroplast

Chloroplasts are small, membrane-bound structures inside plant cells where photosynthesis takes place. Most chloroplasts are found in leaf cells, which are designed for photosynthesis. This process has two main stages, as shown in Figure 4.5. The two key parts of chloroplasts needed for photosynthesis are the grana and the stroma . Grana (singular: granum ) are stacks of coin-shaped compartments called thylakoids ( THY-luh-KOYDZ ). The membranes of the thylakoids contain chlorophyll, other light-absorbing molecules, and proteins. The stroma is the fluid that surrounds the grana inside a chloroplast. ## Stage 1: Capturing Sunlight Energy

The light-dependent reactions are the first part of photosynthesis. They capture energy from sunlight. These reactions happen within and across the membranes of the thylakoids inside the chloroplasts. Both water (H₂O) and sunlight are required for this stage of photosynthesis. Here’s what happens: Chlorophyll absorbs energy from sunlight. This energy moves along the thylakoid membrane. Water molecules (H₂O) are broken down. This process releases oxygen molecules (O₂), which is why plants produce oxygen. The energy that is moved along the thylakoid membrane is transferred to other molecules that carry energy, such as ATP ( adenosine triphosphate ). These energy-carrying molecules will be used in the next stage of photosynthesis. In summary, the light-dependent reactions take light energy, water, and chlorophyll to produce oxygen and energy-carrying molecules like ATP. This step essentially converts light energy into a usable chemical form for the plant.

## Stage 2: Making Sugars and the Photosynthesis Equation

The light-independent reactions , also known as the Calvin cycle , are the second stage of photosynthesis. These reactions use the energy from the light-dependent reactions (carried by molecules like ATP) to make sugars. The light-independent reactions happen in the stroma of the chloroplasts. Carbon dioxide molecules (CO₂) are essential for this stage. Here’s what happens: Carbon dioxide (CO₂) is added to a series of chemical reactions to build larger molecules. The energy from the light-dependent reactions powers these building processes. Eventually, a molecule of a simple sugar is formed. This sugar is typically glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆), which stores some of the energy originally captured from sunlight. The overall equation for photosynthesis summarizes the entire process: $$ (carbon dioxide + water produces a sugar + oxygen) This equation shows that many steps are involved, requiring enzymes and other chemicals, not just light, carbon dioxide, and water. Glucose and other simple sugars, like fructose, are vital for plants. Plants use these simple sugars to build larger molecules such as starch and cellulose, which are important for their growth and structure. The oxygen produced during photosynthesis is also crucial for many organisms, including us.
